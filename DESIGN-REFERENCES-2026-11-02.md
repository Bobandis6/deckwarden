# Design references for Deckwarden (2026-11-02)

**Status:** ideas 1 (phase 1, the grid tile into the hub hero and back) and 2 (the theme reveal) shipped on 2026-11-02 as VIEW-TRANSITIONS, the owner's pick; the record is the REDESIGN.md addendum of that name and LATER rows 210, 222 and 394 to 396. Ideas 3 to 5 and idea 1's hub-to-editor half are still recommendations. Six reference sites were requested; all six were reachable and read on 2026-11-02 (notes on how at the end).

## 1. The read of Deckwarden these picks were filtered through

- **What it is.** A free two-game deck builder (Magic Commander, One Piece) whose promise is the fastest path from picking a commander to a legal, shareable deck, with explainable numbers (brackets, speed facts, prices, tournament evidence) instead of black-box advice.
- **Who it is for.** Commander and One Piece players who already use Moxfield, Archidekt and Scryfall and judge a builder by speed, card art and honest numbers. Keyboard first on desktop, Deck / Search / Tools tabs on phones.
- **Visual language (settled, Wave 5 complete).** "Crafted editorial restraint": Literata for titles, Geist for UI and data, Geist Mono for keycaps. Charcoal dark by default, ivory light. Green action buttons with the gold hairline, gold as the Warden's voice, lavender and teal as game context only. Art is the color; chrome is quiet. The home page is a lit stage with three real daily commanders and two painted game doors; cards are objects on a table (grounded shadows, the 9-degree turn, the 2 px lift).
- **Motion contract (U2).** CSS only, four `linear()` curves: settle-120 for controls, settle-250 for cross-fades, settle-400 for one-off moments, flick-400 only for what a finger threw. Everything `motion-safe:`, a reduced-motion backstop, no entry fades on page-like surfaces, no filter animations (the token test bans `drop-shadow`), no outer glows, no glass except where content scrolls under chrome.
- **Standing decisions I did not relitigate.** The pinned H1 and nav labels, eyebrows for list groups only (F4), no new dependency (F19), One Piece art stays full-card (F20), folders stay rows not tiles (R5b), the hub-to-editor morph waits for its trigger (LATER row 210, which includes "or the owner asks").

## 2. The five

### 1. The card persists across navigation (a shared-element morph on the build path)

- **Reference.** Design Spells 346, "Smooth transitions when browsing looks on Doji": https://designspells.com/spells/smooth-transitions-when-browsing-looks-on-doji (video: https://cdn.designspells.com/videos/doji.mp4). The look you tap in the grid is the same object that fills the next screen; nothing is replaced, it grows. Same family: 325, "3D transition when navigating to a story from a different user on Instagram", https://designspells.com/spells/3d-transition-when-navigating-to-a-story-from-a-different-user-on-instagram. The web pattern, as a component: Aceternity "Expandable Cards", https://ui.aceternity.com/components/expandable-card (a card expands in place into its detail).
- **The detail to borrow.** One object, two pages. The thumbnail does not disappear and a hero appear; the same card moves and scales from its grid cell into the hero slot, and reverses on back.
- **Where and how.** Phase 1, static to static: the tile on `/commanders` and `/leaders` into the hub hero card on `/c/[slug]` and `/l/[slug]`. Wrap the `CardImage` in the grid tile (`src/components/hub/leader-index-view.tsx`, the Grid view both indexes share) and in the hub hero (`src/app/(site)/c/[slug]/page.tsx:171`) in React's `<ViewTransition name={"card-" + printingId}>`. The installed Next 16.4.2 guide (`node_modules/next/dist/docs/01-app/02-guides/view-transitions.md`) confirms this needs no config: route navigations are transitions, React 19.3 ships the component, unsupported browsers simply navigate. Set `share="morph"` and `default="none"` so only the card moves and nothing else fades (the no-entry-fades rule holds). Time the group with settle-400 in `globals.css` (`::view-transition-group(.morph)`), and zero every `::view-transition-*` animation under `prefers-reduced-motion`. Phase 2, the hub hero into the editor's leader zone (the F13 row): only after checking that the editor's leader box renders in the navigation commit, because the guide says a destination that suspends into a fallback first forms no pair. The hub's hero card is also the hero that later tilts (idea 3), so the object the reader handles on the hub is the one that flies into the deck.
- **Why.** The product's promise is the path from a commander to a deck. Today each step is a cut: grid, then hub, then editor, three unrelated screens. Continuity across the cut says "you are building with this card" without a word, and it is the one motion the design lab already named as the place `<ViewTransition>` applies.
- **Cost and risks.** Phase 1 is S (two wrappers and a few CSS lines; ISR pages are prefetched by `Link`, so the pair forms). Phase 2 is M and gated on the pairing check. Safari animates some view transitions differently; the fallback is a plain navigation, never a broken one.

### 2. The theme switch reveals from where you clicked

- **Reference.** Design Spells 146, "Ripple animation when toggling between light and dark modes in HackerRank": https://designspells.com/spells/ripple-animation-when-toggling-between-light-and-dark-modes-in-hackerrank (video: https://cdn.designspells.com/videos/hackerrank.mp4). The new theme sweeps out as a circle from the toggle in the header; the frame at 1.2 s shows the dark circle covering half the page with its center on the sun icon.
- **The detail to borrow.** The change has an origin. Instead of every element easing its own colors (or snapping), one circle grows from the control that caused it.
- **Where and how.** `src/components/theme/appearance-menu.tsx`: when the reader picks Light or Dark, read the trigger's rect, write `--reveal-x` and `--reveal-y` on `<html>`, and call `document.startViewTransition(() => setTheme(next))` where it exists and motion is allowed; otherwise flip at once. CSS in `globals.css`: `::view-transition-old(root)` stays still, `::view-transition-new(root)` animates `clip-path` from `circle(0 at var(--reveal-x) var(--reveal-y))` to a circle that covers the viewport, 400 ms on settle-400. Keep next-themes' `disableTransitionOnChange` (it is now exactly right: nothing else should transition) and retire the U8 body-background ease, whose per-element cost is recorded in LATER row 222. A System-theme change from the OS has no origin, so it flips plainly. The provider stays outside the editor tree, and a view transition snapshots the DOM without touching React state, so the editor's deck-dirty rule (REDESIGN.md §7) is untouched.
- **Why.** The current flip eases only the page background because easing everything cost 45 to 51 ms frames in the editor; the result is a half-eased change. One composited reveal is cheaper than that and reads as deliberate. It also shares idea 1's mechanism and stylesheet, so the two land as one package and one reduced-motion rule.
- **Cost.** S. Chromium, Safari 18 and current Firefox animate it; older browsers get the instant flip they get today.

### 3. The hero cards and the leader card answer the pointer (a small tilt)

- **Reference.** Aceternity "3D Card Effect": https://ui.aceternity.com/components/3d-card-effect ("a card perspective effect, hover over the card to elevate card elements"). The phone analog, which I do not recommend copying, is Design Spells "Card preview tilts as you move your phone in Craft": https://designspells.com/spells/card-preview-tilts-as-you-move-your-phone-in-craft (device motion needs a permission tap on iOS).
- **The detail to borrow.** Only the pointer-following rotation, a few degrees about both axes, with the shadow moving the opposite way. Not Aceternity's floating child layers, not a glare (a glare reads as foil and would lie on non-foil printings), no glow.
- **Where and how.** Three places, fine pointers only, desktop only: the three hero cards on home (`src/components/home/hero-fan.tsx`, inside the cell so the stage's 9-degree turn on the cell and the artist-line rule are untouched), the hub hero card on `/c/` and `/l/`, and the editor's leader card in `src/components/editor/deck-list-pane.tsx`. One `pointermove` listener per card writes `--tilt-x` and `--tilt-y` as CSS variables (no React state, no re-render); the transform is `rotateX(var(--tilt-x)) rotateY(var(--tilt-y))`, capped at 6 degrees on home and 4 in the editor, with the existing grounded box-shadow offset by up to 4 px against the tilt. Enter and leave settle over 120 ms on settle-120; the follow itself is a 120 ms transition so the card lags the pointer by a frame and reads as weight. `motion-safe:` and `pointer-fine:` gate it; on touch and under reduced motion the card keeps today's static turn. On home it starts only after the entrance sequence ends (LATER row 298's gate), so the two never fight.
- **Why.** The home stage, the grounded shadows and the 9-degree turn already say "cards on a table"; a card that turns toward the hand completes the sentence and is the affordance for the one action the page wants (click it, build with it). It is a signature moment in the catalog's sense: three places, never on list rows.
- **Cost.** S to M. Transforms only, composited, no new dependency.

### 4. Numbers roll when an action changes them

- **Reference.** Aceternity "Speed Counter": https://ui.aceternity.com/interactions/speed-counter ("a number whose digits race sideways to the new value... commas, decimals, signs, currency and locales stay right"). The static typographic form of the same idea is 24fps's stat band on https://24fps.dev/ (mono label, large tabular numeral, one line of provenance).
- **The detail to borrow.** The digits move and nothing else does: fixed-width tabular columns, each digit rolling to its new value, currency and separators staying put. Not the sideways smear.
- **Where and how.** Deckwarden already has `useCountUp` (`src/components/editor/use-count-up.ts`, R3 F10): the deck total rolls after an import when the delta is above 5, and a plus-or-minus-one edit jumps. Build a `RollingNumber` on that hook's sampler (settle-250, 250 ms) that renders per-digit vertical rolls, then use it in three places where a number is the feedback for the action: the price line when a swap lands in `fit-sheet.tsx`, `target-fit-sheet.tsx` and `swap-sheet.tsx` (Budget Twin, Swap to fit, Swap Lab); the bracket number in `src/components/deck/bracket-line.tsx` when the bracket changes; the deck total in the status slot under today's threshold rule. Reduced motion stays instant (the hook already checks).
- **Why.** "Honest numbers" is the brand, and in the swap flows the number is the whole point: you swapped, the price fell, the bracket held. A number that rolls to its new value is the quietest possible confirmation and needs no toast.
- **Cost.** S. Reuses the existing curve sampling and threshold rules.

### 5. The search shows what it understands

- **Reference.** 24fps's hero search on https://24fps.dev/: under the box, a "Try" line of real links (logo reveals, kinetic type, product launch, explainers, 9:16, each a working `/explore?cat=...` query). Cosmos's explore search on https://www.cosmos.so/explore: the placeholder is a real example, "Try 'vitsoe shelving'", changing per load.
- **The detail to borrow.** Examples are links, not decoration, and they teach the syntax the box accepts.
- **Where and how.** The `/cards` first screen (`src/components/cards/card-search.tsx`, under the filter row, before any query): one "Try" line of four chips that are real `/cards?q=...` links: two of the day's hero commanders (home already picks three per UTC day, so no new query), one One Piece card id (the search already resolves id prefixes), one staple such as Sol Ring. The header search popup's empty state (`src/components/search/header-search.tsx`, before typing) gets the same line in its small form. Optional, per Cosmos: the `/cards` placeholder names one of the day's commanders ("Try Syr Konrad, the Grim"), server-rendered and changing daily, never animated. Copy follows U9 (no dashes, no new eyebrow); the `/` keycap hint stays.
- **Why.** The header search is three weeks old and on every page, and the site has two syntaxes (names, One Piece ids plus the editor's quick-add form) that nothing on screen teaches. Four chips shorten the first query and show that One Piece ids work, with zero motion and no new data.
- **Cost.** S.

## 3. How the five fit together

- Ideas 1 and 2 are one mechanism (view transitions), one stylesheet block and one reduced-motion rule.
- Ideas 1 and 3 treat the card as the persistent object: the card you tilt on the hub is the card that flies into the deck.
- Ideas 4 and 5 serve the two promises the audience judges a builder by: honest numbers and speed to a first result.
- All five keep the contract: CSS-driven, inside 120 / 250 / 400, `motion-safe:`, no entry fades, no glows, no glass, no dependency, no pinned string moved.

Suggested packaging, one package per session as usual: (a) view transitions, ideas 1 phase 1 and 2; (b) the table and the numbers, ideas 3 and 4; (c) search examples, idea 5. Idea 1 phase 2 waits on the pairing check.

## 4. What I looked at and left, per site

- **24fps.dev.** The header timecode that advances with scroll and the orange progress line are witty but readouts for their own sake; a gold reading-progress hairline on `/brackets` would be the honest version if wanted (S, scroll-driven CSS). Mono uppercase eyebrows conflict with the eyebrow decision. The heavy sans display and marker underline do not fit Literata.
- **raivcoo.com.** Hover-to-play (no video on Deckwarden), glass chips over artwork (the glass rule), crop-mark section frames (decoration), masonry (cards share one ratio).
- **ui.aceternity.com.** Nearly everything else is against the anti-slop notes: beams, aurora, sparkles, meteors, moving borders, text-generate fades, focus cards (blur is a filter animation), Glare Card (reads as foil).
- **designspells.com.** Easter eggs and minigames exceed the fun ceiling. The sheet spells (X, Are.na, Sudoku) are already covered by the phone card sheet's snap points and flick release. Gentler Streak's effort picker (a scale annotated with "your usual range") is a good later idea for the bracket target scale, but it is a feature, not polish.
- **cosmos.so.** The scattered floating hero (home has its stage), the explore masonry, the three-image collection tiles (folders stay rows, R5b), color search.
- **motionindex.io.** The search that docks into the header on scroll (the header already carries search), measured facets shown as picture tiles (a possible form for the brackets guide, not polish), the floating "Showing 24 of 14,367" count pill (an alternate for `/precons`, which is 23k px on phones).

## 5. How the sites were read

- Text of every site through fetches; layout and interaction in a headless Chrome at 1440 x 900 through the DevTools MCP, because the in-app browser pane was hidden during the session and a hidden pane pauses frame-driven content (24fps's reels rendered blank there).
- Design Spells spells are videos; their frames were read by seeking (`doji.mp4` at 3 s, `hackerrank.mp4` at 1.2 s). Raivcoo's tile hover was triggered with synthetic events (video fades in over 200 ms, thumbnail scales 1.05, glass chips appear). Cosmos's tile hover did not respond to synthetic events and was not observed; nothing in the five depends on it.
- No site was automated against beyond reading its public pages. Nothing from any site enters the repo.
